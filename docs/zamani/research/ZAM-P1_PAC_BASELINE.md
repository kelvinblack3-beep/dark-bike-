# ZAM-P1 — PAC Baseline (Plasma-Assisted Combustion)

Summary

Plasma-Assisted Combustion (PAC) is recorded here as the only currently retained near-term plasma-related technology for Project Zamani. PAC is an ignition/combustion-assist technology that uses non-equilibrium plasmas (e.g., nanosecond pulsed discharges) to modify ignition chemistry, produce reactive radicals, enhance flame kernel growth, and potentially enable leaner combustion or higher EGR tolerance.

Role
- CHEMICAL ENGINE + PLASMA ASSIST (NOT A PLASMA ENGINE)

Proposed mechanism
- Non-equilibrium plasma produces reactive species (radicals/ions) and localized heating
- Enhances ignition and flame propagation
- Potentially extends lean limit and improves combustion stability
- Potential to reduce fuel consumption under suitable calibration

Status
- RETAINED (PROVISIONAL) — PAC is an assist technology and remains a candidate for integration only after verification.

Numerical claims from P1 (PROVISIONAL CLAIMS REQUIRING SOURCE-LEVEL VERIFICATION)
- Claimed efficiency improvement range: 2–10% (UNVERIFIED)
- Reported BSFC improvement example: 6% on a 2.5 L Atkinson engine (UNVERIFIED until primary source located)
- Reported fuel-consumption reduction example: 9.4% (UNVERIFIED)
- Plasma electrical consumption claim: <1 kW (PROVISIONAL / UNVERIFIED)
- PAC subsystem mass claim: 10–25 kg (ENGINEERING ESTIMATE / UNVERIFIED)
- Claimed TRL: 6–7 (SOURCE-DEPENDENT / UNVERIFIED)

Recording policy
- All numerical claims above remain UNVERIFIED until traced to primary literature, data sheets, or reproducible experiments. See ZAM-P1_CLAIM_VERIFICATION_REGISTER.md for tracking.

Validation pathway (summary)
- Locate primary-source experimental reports and datasheets
- Reproduce first-principles energy accounting (plasma power vs combustion energy)
- Build a BSFC and engine-efficiency model including plasma electrical input and losses
- Durability assessment of ignition hardware at engine speeds and temperatures
- EMI and controls integration study

Notes
- PAC may remain an evolutionary efficiency enhancement rather than a breakthrough. Do not promote PAC as a vehicle primary power generator.
